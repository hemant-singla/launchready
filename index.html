    Under the hood How the verdict is calculated

    Software, per chipset

    Testing starts when all code is in and test boards exist. The ready date is a range: the test window (14 days, or 28 for a new chipset) plus a stabilisation tail, or the time to clear open tickets (critical 3–14 days, major 2–7, minor 1–3, shared across each team's engineers), whichever is later. Ready means the latest date is at least 7 days before launch, with no late code and no open critical tickets. Blocked means even the earliest date is after launch.

    Supply, per model

    Parts are handed out in need-date order: test boards before testing starts, production parts 21 days before launch. Each unit goes to only one build. Rejected deliveries and deliveries with no date are never counted. At risk means a late or tight delivery, an approved substitute, or a delivery with no date. Blocked means parts are missing, or production parts are more than 7 days late.

    AI agent

    From a change to an approved plan

    When something changes, the agent looks into both sides using tools (lookups and calculations in code), weighs options, proposes a plan with trade-offs, drafts messages to owners, and waits for your approval. Each run shown here is a real agent run, recorded during the build and replayed; nothing is generated live.
