Will each TV launch on time? One view for software and supply.

LaunchReady joins the two sides of a TV launch. It shows each component team's code and open tickets, plus parts, stock and supplier deliveries. Every model gets a Ready, At risk or Blocked verdict, with the reasons from both sides.

Fictional data. Northwind Vision, its 16 TVs, 8 chipsets, suppliers and tickets are all made up. This is a demo, not a deployed tool, with no real users or savings. Every date and quantity on screen is calculated by the code in this repo, not typed in by hand or guessed by AI.

How the verdict is calculated

Software, per chipset

Testing starts when all code is in and test boards exist. The ready date is a range: test window (14 days, or 28 for a new chipset) plus a stabilisation tail, or the time to clear open tickets (critical 3–14 days, major 2–7, minor 1–3, shared across each team's engineers), whichever is later. Ready means the latest date is at least 7 days before launch, with no late code and no open critical tickets. Blocked means even the earliest date is after launch.

Supply, per model

Parts are handed out in need-date order (test boards before testing starts, production parts 21 days before launch). Each unit goes to only one build. Deliveries that were rejected or have no date are never counted. At risk means a late or tight delivery, an approved substitute, or a delivery with no date. Blocked means parts are missing or more than 7 days late for production.
