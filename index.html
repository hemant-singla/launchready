    What do these terms mean?
    Chipset
    The main processor family a TV is built on. Several models share one chipset, and its software is tested once for all of them.
    Test boards
    Early circuit boards used to test the software before production. No boards, no testing.
    7-day buffer
    The software must be ready at least 7 days before launch. That date is the cutoff.
    Date range
    Software dates are shown as earliest–latest, because fixing bugs is uncertain. A launch is ready only if even the latest date meets the cutoff.
    Re-peg
    Move parts from an order bought for another chipset to the one that needs them. Same part only, plus 2 days to transfer.
    Substitute part
    An approved alternative component. It needs a driver change and extra validation before testing.
    About this prototype

    What LaunchReady is, and isn't

      Who it's for

      Launch managers, supply planners and software leads at a TV maker who meet weekly to decide whether each model can launch on its date.

      The decision it supports

      Go / no-go per model, and when a supplier slips: which launches are hit, what can recover them, and what needs escalating.

      Why the data is fictional

      Real launch, supplier and ticket data is confidential. "Northwind Vision", its models, chipsets and suppliers are invented so the logic can be shown in full. This is a portfolio prototype: not deployed, no users, no claimed savings.

      Built vs proposed

      Built and tested: the readiness engine, supply allocation, scenarios, the what-if lab, recovery options, and the simulated agent workflow. Proposed, not built: live connectors to an issue tracker and ERP, and recorded AI-agent runs (the tool-calling code is in agent/ but no runs are recorded).

      Assumptions How the verdict is calculated

      Software, per chipset

      Testing starts when all code is in and test boards exist. The ready date is a range (earliest–latest): the test window (14 days, or 28 for a new chipset) plus a stabilisation tail, or the time to clear open tickets (critical 3–14 days, major 2–7, minor 1–3, shared across each team's engineers), whichever is later. Ready means even the latest date is at least 7 days before launch (the buffer), with no late code and no open critical tickets. Blocked means even the earliest date is after launch.

      Supply, per model

      Parts are handed out in need-date order: test boards before testing starts, production parts 21 days before launch. Each unit goes to only one build. Rejected deliveries and deliveries with no date are never counted. At risk means a late or tight delivery, a substitute, or a delivery with no date. Blocked means parts are missing, or production parts are more than 7 days late.

      Recovery

      Options are only offered when the data allows them: re-pegging an order bought for another program (same part, +2 days transfer), an approved substitute (driver change 1–14 days before testing, then 2–4 days of validation), or a later launch (up to 42 days). Each is tested with the engine; any option that makes another launch worse is dropped. Data as of .
